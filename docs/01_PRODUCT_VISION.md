# Product Vision and Non-Goals

## High concept
A tiny living Bangladeshi landscape begins parched. Rain arrives. The player carves channels and builds a delicate network of water systems that turns empty ground into a thriving village. Every improvement is **visible on the map**: a field becomes lush, a wheel spins, windows light up, fish swim, flowers bloom.

## Emotional pillars
1. **Tactile satisfaction**: drawing and connecting canals should feel as smooth as doodling.
2. **Cause and effect**: water physically arrives at destinations; buildings do not work by magic.
3. **Gentle discovery**: a few systems combine into surprising, intelligible outcomes.
4. **Restoration**: visual progress is stronger than score inflation.
5. **Bangladesh authenticity**: rice paddies, bamboo bridges, tin-roof homes, ponds, shāpla, rickshaw colors, monsoon skies, floating gardens. Avoid caricature and disaster trivialization.
6. **Mobile parity**: fully playable by touch, mouse and optionally keyboard.

## Target audience
Bangladeshi university-age players and anyone who enjoys cozy strategy, sandbox landscapes, mini-simulations, environmental puzzles. All genders; no stereotypes.

## User fantasy
'I drew a little ditch, and now an entire village is alive because of it.'

## Session shape
First satisfying result in 20–40 seconds; first visible crop growth within 1–2 minutes; first turbine and lit house in 5–8 minutes; optional 15–30+ minute sandbox. These are targets to tune, not fixed promises.

## Win condition
No losing screen, hard time limit or competitive scoring. Milestones culminate in **Village in Bloom**, after which endless sandbox play remains available. Water scarcity and flooding cause reversible setbacks, not permanent punishment.

## Never do
Do not require logins; never gate the core play behind ads; do not require a hosted API; avoid fake scientific claims; do not hide essential interactions behind mouse hover; no autoplay loud audio; no pay-to-win.

## Scope priorities
P0: flow/draw/feedback/saving/responsive; P1: farms, reservoir, power, purification, fish, progression; P2: habitat and decoration; P3: experimental systems.
