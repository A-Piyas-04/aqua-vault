# Science, Fiction, and Bangladeshi Authenticity

## Scientific grounding
- Gravity drives downhill water; canals change routing and resistance.
- Hydropower depends on flow **and head** (height difference); energy is never produced by a still reservoir alone.
- Energy can pump water uphill, but needs a cost; not free energy.
- Rainwater from roofs or surfaces may carry germs/chemicals. Depict layered treatment (filter and disinfect); boiling kills many pathogens but **does not remove toxic chemical contaminants**. Never present game's schematic as real-world advice for potable-water safety.
- Fish need a suitable pond and water-quality conditions; drinking water and fish habitat are different needs.
- Rice paddies and reservoirs consume/store water; ecological wetlands can benefit from shallow flows.

## Fiction label
Micro-turbines and compact treatment plants are **science-fiction miniaturizations**. Output rates and time scales are intentionally accelerated for fun; no numeric game unit purports to represent liters, kWh or agricultural yield without calibration. Display tiny 'Inspired by real science; simplified for play' in About panel.

## Distinctly Bangladeshi elements
Rural canals (খাল), pond (পুকুর), rain (বৃষ্টি), rice field (ধানক্ষেত), water lily (শাপলা), bamboo footbridge (সাঁকো), tin roof homes, banana plants, ducks/frogs, beautiful skies, patterned boat/canoe motifs. The FAO recognizes floating cultivation systems in Bangladesh, especially southern waterlogged regions; include floating beds as a late unlock with simple educational flavor.

## Sensitivity
Avoid political/religious references, exploitative disaster scenes or stereotypes. Treat rural life with beauty and care rather than ridicule. Avoid implying Bangladesh is uniformly rural or uniformly water-scarce; this is one fictional village during a particular season.
